### OWN ANALYSIS: does "spreading rumors to make the buyback cheaper" hold up?

The evidence weighs heavily against it:

- **Public and long-running:** the program was announced with every authorization and has run for years.
- **Buying near the highs:** NVIDIA bought about $14B at or near record prices before the drop.
- **Sector-wide fall:** the 5 Nov decline hit all chip stocks, and the semiconductor index fell more than NVDA.

**4. Legally this would be a serious crime, not a gray area.** (Confidence: high on the law; this is not legal advice)

- The Rule 10b-18 safe harbor for buybacks only covers how purchases are made: the manner, timing, price and volume of trades (for example, the 25%-of-average-daily-volume cap). It gives no protection to a scheme to depress the price.
- Buying back stock while knowing a planted rumor is false would also be trading on material nonpublic information.
- Reg FD forbids selective disclosure, so a quiet leak to chosen parties would be another violation.
- Timing works against the thesis too. NVIDIA's Q3 FY27 ended in late October and results are due 2026-11-17. The 10-29 report therefore landed in the usual pre-earnings quiet window, when buybacks typically run through pre-set 10b5-1 plans whose purchases are fixed in advance. ### Does the "manipulation" theory hold? (my analysis)

The theory says NVIDIA is pushing bad news to buy back shares cheaper. The evidence points the other way:

- The story's source is US intelligence leaking to the NYT, and Huang confirmed the affair himself. NVIDIA spent a week denying it and then had to admit it. That is costly to the company, not something it would stage.
- Buyback pace is about $20B a quarter, roughly $0.3B a trading day. That is a small fraction of NVDA's daily trading value.
- SEC Rule 10b-18 caps a company's daily buyback volume at 25% of average daily trading volume. Deliberately spreading false negative news would be securities fraud.

There is a real information-war angle, but it runs in a different direction:

- The leak landed as Congress was pushing the AI Overwatch Act and challenging H200 licenses, and before the 17 Nov earnings and 18 Nov proposed briefing.
- It strengthens export-control hawks in Washington and weakens Huang's lobbying for China access.
- Beijing's "smear" framing is its standard response.
- A suspected Chinese intelligence asset getting close to a CEO, then exposed through US intelligence leaks, fits a broader US–China counterintelligence pattern.
- Whoever leaked the story, its main effect is on policy and governance. The buyback is incidental.

**Impact on NVDA:** If H200 licenses were revoked, guidance would lose almost nothing (under 1% of data center revenue). The larger risks are:
- CEO distraction or a leave of absence, given how dependent the company is on Huang.
- Stricter rules on sales to Southeast Asian customers that bring diversion risk, such as Megaspeed. NVIDIA called Megaspeed its biggest Southeast Asian customer.
- Disclosure and liability exposure if the board finds misleading statements.

## On the "manipulation to lower the buyback price" hypothesis (my analysis)

**There is no technical evidence for it.**
- Most of the drawdown, from $239 to $181, came before Nov 9 and was sector-wide. Peers without any scandal fell more.
- The Huang story is VERIFIED as widely reported by major outlets (CNBC, CNN, Al Jazeera, The Hill, Nov 9):
  - a board investigation;
  - a reported admission by Huang of a relationship;
  - the claim that the woman passed information to Beijing, which reports attribute to US intelligence agencies.
- I have **not independently verified** the espionage claim itself. The board reportedly said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.
- The buyback is real and ongoing. SEC 10-Q filings show $39.8B repurchased in H1 FY2027 and an $80B authorization added May 18, 2026. A further $150B (Sep 28, 2026) is REPORTED via Reuters / Khaleej Times; I did not confirm it in a filing.
- A buyback that runs continuously does not show anyone is spreading negative news on purpose.
